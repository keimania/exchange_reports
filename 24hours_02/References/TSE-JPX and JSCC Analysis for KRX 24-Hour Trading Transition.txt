1. CURRENT TRADING HOURS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Since November 5, 2024, the TSE cash equity market operates under the following
schedule (all times JST):

  Pre-Opening Order Acceptance:  08:00 - 09:00
  Morning Session (Zenba):      09:00 - 11:30 (Zaraba continuous trading)
  Lunch Break:                   11:30 - 12:30
  Afternoon Session (Goba):     12:30 - 15:25 (Zaraba continuous trading)
  Pre-Closing Session:          15:25 - 15:30 (order acceptance only, no execution)
  Closing Auction (Itayose):    15:30 (single-price determination)

  Total Continuous Trading Time: 5 hours 25 minutes per day

KEY 2024 CHANGE: On November 5, 2024, the TSE extended afternoon trading by 30
minutes (from the previous close at 15:00 to 15:25) and introduced a 5-minute
Closing Auction session. This was the first extension of market close in 70 years
(since 1954) and the first trading hours change in 13 years.

CLOSING AUCTION MECHANISM (introduced Nov 5, 2024):
  - At 15:25, Zaraba (continuous) trading halts
  - 15:25-15:30: Pre-closing session where only orders are accepted; no matching
    occurs. Order book information is disseminated but prices remain unchanged.
  - Investors can place new orders, modify existing orders, or cancel orders
  - At 15:30: Itayose (call auction) determines the closing price
  - Closing price must fall within a range of twice the "renewal price interval"
    from the last traded price at 15:25
  - Example: For a stock at JPY 100 with a 5-yen interval, closing price range
    would be JPY 90-110
  - "Funari" orders (limit orders that convert to market orders for the closing
    auction if unfilled) and "on-close" orders are automatically registered

RATIONALE: Passive index-tracking now represents approximately 15% of daily
volume on TSE, creating heavy demand concentration at market close. The closing
auction improves transparency and fairness of closing price formation.

OSAKA EXCHANGE (OSE) CORRESPONDING CHANGES:
  - Intraday derivatives closing shifted to 15:45
  - Night session opening shifted to 17:00

3. PRE-MARKET AND AFTER-HOURS TRADING (ToSTNeT SYSTEM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ToSTNeT (Tokyo Stock Exchange Trading NeTwork System) provides off-auction
trading capabilities outside the regular continuous trading sessions.

TYPES OF ToSTNeT TRADING:
  - ToSTNeT-1: Single-issue basket trading
    * Designated counterpart transactions
    * Price must be within +/- 7% of last regular session price
    * Minimum: one trading unit
  - ToSTNeT-2: Portfolio basket trading
    * For basket trades of multiple issues
  - ToSTNeT-3: Treasury stock repurchase system
    * Buy side exclusively reserved for the listed company itself
    * Sell orders distributed according to TSE allocation methods
    * Executed at 08:45 (before market open at 09:00)
    * Company announces program around 16:30 after afternoon session

ToSTNeT TRADING HOURS:
  Session 1: 08:20 - 09:00 (pre-market)
  Session 2: 11:30 - 12:30 (lunch break)
  Session 3: 15:30 - 16:00 (post-close, adjusted from previous 15:00)

NOTE: ToSTNeT is for negotiated trades with predetermined counterparties, not
for anonymous continuous matching. It serves institutional needs for large-lot
and basket transactions.

4. ORDER TYPES SUPPORTED ON TSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

BASIC ORDER TYPES:
  - Limit Order (指値注文): Specify maximum buy / minimum sell price
  - Market Order (成行注文): Execute at best available price

EXECUTION CONDITIONS:
  - Day Order: Valid for current trading day only
  - IOC (Immediate-or-Cancel): Execute immediately at limit or better; cancel
    any unfilled portion
  - Note: FOK (Fill-or-Kill) and Iceberg orders are NOT available on TSE
    (these are available on PTS venues like Japannext)

SPECIAL ORDER TYPES:
  - Funari Order (引成注文): Submitted as limit order during continuous trading;
    converts to market order at the closing auction if unfilled
  - Limit-on-Close (LOC): Limit order executed only during closing auction
  - Market-on-Close (MOC): Market order submitted for closing auction
  - Limit-on-Open (LOO): Limit order for opening auction only
  - Market-on-Open (MOO): Market order for opening auction only

ORDER PRIORITY RULES:
  1. Price Priority: Better-priced orders execute first (lower sell / higher buy)
  2. Time Priority: Earlier orders at the same price execute first (except during
     Itayose auctions, where all orders are treated as simultaneous)

SHORT SELLING:
  - Uptick rule applies: Short sale price must be at least 1 tick higher than
    the last traded price

5. VOLATILITY CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TSE employs a multi-layered volatility control framework:

A) DAILY PRICE LIMITS (値幅制限):
   The TSE sets absolute yen price limits based on the previous day's closing
   price (Base Price). Stocks cannot trade beyond these limits.

   Sample Price Limit Table:
   Base Price Range          Daily Limit (each direction)
   Under JPY 200             JPY 80
   JPY 200 - 500             JPY 100
   JPY 500 - 700             JPY 150
   JPY 700 - 1,000           JPY 200
   JPY 1,000 - 1,500         JPY 300
   JPY 1,500 - 2,000         JPY 400
   JPY 2,000 - 3,000         JPY 500
   JPY 3,000 - 5,000         JPY 700
   JPY 5,000 - 7,000         JPY 1,000
   JPY 7,000 - 10,000        JPY 1,500
   JPY 10,000 - 15,000       JPY 3,000
   (continues for higher price ranges)

   PRICE LIMIT EXPANSION: Limits widen on the third business day if, for two
   consecutive days:
   (1) Price reaches the upper/lower limit AND no closing auction at that limit
       price is carried out AND trading volume remains zero; OR
   (2) No shares traded until end of afternoon session, but some traded at the
       closing auction at the limit price with bids/offers remaining at the limit.

B) SPECIAL QUOTES (特別気配, Tokubetsu Kehai):
   When prices are likely to jump beyond the "special quote renewal price
   interval" from the last execution price, a special quote is indicated and
   continuous trading is temporarily halted.

   Mechanism:
   - A special quote is displayed when order imbalance would cause the next
     execution to exceed the renewal price interval
   - The special quote price is updated every 3 minutes in the direction of
     the imbalance, moving by the renewal price interval each time
   - This provides time for market participants to submit offsetting orders
   - Once sufficient orders accumulate, an Itayose (call auction) determines
     the new price
   - Example: Stock at JPY 500 with 10-yen interval - next execution must be
     within JPY 490-510; if it would exceed this, a special quote is triggered

   Renewal Price Intervals (sample):
   Last Price Range           Renewal Interval
   Under JPY 200              JPY 5
   JPY 200 - 500              JPY 5-10
   JPY 500 - 1,000            JPY 10
   JPY 1,000 - 2,000          JPY 20-30
   JPY 5,000 - 10,000         JPY 50-100

C) CIRCUIT BREAKERS:
   Two types operate on TSE:

   1. Static Circuit Breaker (Price Limits / Circuit Breaker Rule):
      - Applied to index futures and options on OSE
      - Temporarily halts all trading when the market moves excessively
      - Based on predefined absolute price change thresholds

   2. Dynamic Circuit Breaker (Immediately Executable Price Range):
      - Similar to the special quotation mechanism in cash equities
      - Prevents execution outside a dynamic price range around the last
        traded price
      - Triggers a brief trading halt and call auction

COMPARISON TO KRX:
  KRX uses Volatility Interruption (VI) as its primary mechanism, which
  triggers a 2-minute call auction on individual stocks. TSE's special quote
  system is conceptually similar but operates with a gradual price discovery
  mechanism (3-minute renewal cycles) rather than a fixed-duration call auction.

6. EXTENDED HOURS / 24-HOUR TRADING DISCUSSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CURRENT STATE OF JPX EQUITY TRADING HOURS:
  - Cash equities: 09:00-15:30 (5h 25m) after November 2024 extension
  - No nighttime session for cash equities exists

JPX DERIVATIVES NIGHT SESSION (existing):
  - OSE/TOCOM Night Session: 17:00 - 05:30 (next day) JST
  - Night session captures >40% of total derivatives volume (2024: 168.5
    million contracts, up from 39% ratio)
  - Timeline of extensions:
    September 2007: Evening session launched (16:30-19:00)
    2011: Extended to 03:00, renamed "Night Session"
    July 2016: Extended to 05:30 (overlaps 20:30 London / 14:30 Chicago)
  - Nikkei 225 mini has become the third most actively traded index futures
    globally, largely driven by night session popularity
  - Foreign investors dominate night session; Japanese retail participation
    growing via online brokerages

EQUITY EXTENDED HOURS PLANS:
  As of March 2026, JPX has NOT announced concrete plans for nighttime cash
  equity trading sessions or 24-hour equity trading. The November 2024
  extension to 15:30 was described as a significant step, but further
  extension plans have not been publicly formalized.

  Key considerations:
  - The derivatives night session demonstrates proven demand for extended hours
  - Clearing and settlement infrastructure (JSCC, JASDEC, BOJ-NET) operates
    on T+2 batch processing cycles that would require fundamental redesign
  - PTS venues (Japannext) already provide de facto extended hours for equities
    (08:20-16:30 daytime, 17:00-06:00 nighttime)
  - The "division of labor" between TSE (primary venue) and PTS (extended hours)
    may be JPX's preferred model rather than TSE itself going 24-hour

7. PTS (PROPRIETARY TRADING SYSTEMS) LANDSCAPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Japan's PTS framework was established under the Financial System Reformation
Law of 1998, allowing securities firms to operate electronic trading systems
outside traditional stock exchanges.

A) JAPANNEXT (SBI JAPANNEXT)
   Established: 2006
   Founders: SBI Securities, Rakuten Securities, Orix Securities, Click Securities
   Market Share: ~5% of Japanese equity trading (second to TSE's ~90%+)
   Technology: NASDAQ matching technology (deployed September 26, 2011)

   TRADING HOURS (JST):
     J-Market (Daytime):  08:20 - 16:30
     X-Market (Daytime):  08:20 - 16:30 (separate matching with DLP rules)
     U-Market (Daytime):  08:20 - 16:30 (index arbitrage with flexible tick sizes)
     N-Market (Night):    17:00 - 06:00

   Total Coverage: 08:20-06:00 (next day) = ~21.5 hours

   MATCHING METHOD: Continuous matching based on price-time priority
   No pre-open or closing auctions conducted

   ORDER TYPES:
     - Limit Orders
     - IOC (Immediate-or-Cancel)
     - FOK (Fill-or-Kill)
     - Short Sell / Short Sell Exempt

   PROTOCOLS: OUCH, ITCH, FIX

   RISK CONTROLS:
     - Fat Finger Limits: Max order quantity = 5% of outstanding shares
     - Max order value: JPY 100 million (extendable to JPY 2.5 billion)
     - Price range restrictions

   SIGNIFICANCE FOR KRX COMPARISON:
   Japannext's Night Market (17:00-06:00) provides Japanese equity trading
   coverage that overlaps with US and European market hours. This is analogous
   to what Nextrade provides in Korea (08:00-20:00) but with significantly
   longer overnight hours.

B) CBOE JAPAN (CEASED OPERATIONS)
   Status: Cboe Global Markets announced discontinuation of Japanese equities
   business; operations ended August 29, 2025.

   Former Trading Hours: 08:20-16:00

   Background: In August 2024, Cboe acquired a minority stake in Japannext
   from SBI Holdings before deciding to close its own PTS operations.

   IMPACT: With Cboe Japan's closure, TSE is effectively the only multilateral
   exchange venue in Japan, with Japannext as the sole remaining PTS. This
   concentration raises questions about competition and extended-hours access.

1. CCP ROLE AND STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CORPORATE OVERVIEW:
  - Full Name: Japan Securities Clearing Corporation (日本証券クリアリング機構)
  - Status: Wholly-owned subsidiary of Japan Exchange Group, Inc. (JPX)
  - Role: Central Counterparty (CCP) for Japanese financial markets
  - Regulatory Status: Licensed as a Financial Instruments Clearing Organization
    under Japan's Financial Instruments and Exchange Act (FIEA)

CLEARING BUSINESSES (Products Cleared):
  1. Cash Products: Listed equities, ETFs, REITs
  2. Listed Derivatives: Futures and options on OSE and TOCOM
  3. Listed Commodity Derivatives: Merged into JSCC in July 2020
  4. OTC Derivatives - IRS: Interest Rate Swaps clearing
  5. OTC Derivatives - CDS: Credit Default Swaps clearing
  6. OTC JGB: Japanese Government Bond transactions

KEY STATISTICS (2024):
  - IRS clearing volume doubled in 2024 vs. 2023, driven by Japan's changing
    interest-rate environment
  - 80% of domestic JGB trades cleared at JSCC (despite no regulatory mandate)

PARTICIPANTS:
  - As of February 2026, 32+ domestic securities firms participate
  - International clearing members include major global banks

2. SETTLEMENT CYCLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CURRENT SETTLEMENT CYCLES:
  - Listed Equities: T+2 (trade date basis) - implemented July 16, 2019
  - Japanese Government Bonds (domestic): T+1 - implemented May 1, 2018
  - Japanese Government Bonds (cross-border): T+2
  - Corporate Bonds: T+2

T+1 MIGRATION FOR EQUITIES - STATUS:
  As of March 2026, Japan has NOT set a concrete timeline for equity T+1
  migration. The situation is as follows:

  - The Financial System Council's Working Group on Market Systems has been
    discussing T+1 migration since 2024
  - The July 2024 report "Toward Establishing Product Governance, etc."
    indicated a policy of "beginning practical consideration of approaches
    and issues for T+1 migration"
  - JFSA and industry bodies have begun "informal discussions on the potential
    impacts of a shift for Japanese securities to T+1"
  - No formal transition date or schedule has been announced

  KEY CHALLENGES IDENTIFIED:
  - 50% of settlement fails in Japan are reportedly due to late instructions,
    meaning T+2 is already insufficient time for many participants
  - Fundamental market structure change would be required (messaging, funding,
    reconciliation gaps)
  - Japan's current settlement model is "highly unlikely to survive a move to
    T+1" without major infrastructure overhaul (Broadridge analysis)
  - Pre-settlement matching via JASDEC's PSMS system would need acceleration
  - Cross-border settlement timelines present particular challenges

  GLOBAL CONTEXT:
  - US: T+1 implemented May 28, 2024
  - UK: T+1 targeted for October 11, 2027
  - EU: T+1 targeted for October 11, 2027
  - South Korea (KRX): Planning T+1 alongside 24-hour trading transition

3. CLEARING HOURS AND BATCH PROCESSING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DAILY CLEARING TIMELINE (for equities, all times JST):

  Trading Day:
    09:00 - 15:30    Trading on TSE (trades submitted to JSCC for clearing)
    After close       JSCC processes netting calculations

  Settlement Day (T+2):
    07:00 - 12:30    Securities settlement window at JASDEC
    By 12:20         Pre-settlement matching deadline (via JASDEC PSMS)
    13:00            Settlement Cutoff Time for securities delivery
                     (delivering participants must transfer securities to
                     JSCC account at JASDEC)
                     Net paying participants deposit provisional funds
                     settlement amount as cash collateral
    13:00 - 14:15    Securities received from JSCC account
    14:15            Cutoff for additional fund payment (cash collateral)
    15:10            Cash settlement deadline for participants
    15:30            JDCC completes cash settlement
    17:00            Latest fund confirmation

  Failed Settlement:
    - If delivery not completed by 13:00, postponed to next business day
    - Failed settlement is netted with next day's transactions

NETTING PROCESS:
  - JSCC performs multilateral netting on a product-by-product basis
  - Calculates net balance of selling/buying volume per participant
  - Cash payment amounts are netted across products
  - Net securities transfer instructions sent to JASDEC
  - Net funds transfer instructions sent to settlement banks or BOJ

IMPLICATIONS FOR EXTENDED HOURS:
  The current batch-oriented clearing schedule with fixed cutoff times
  (13:00 securities, 14:15 cash) is designed around a single daily trading
  session ending by 15:30. Extended or 24-hour trading would require:
  - Multiple netting cycles or continuous netting
  - Extended settlement bank operating hours
  - Modified JASDEC processing windows
  - Potential real-time or near-real-time clearing capabilities

4. MARGIN AND COLLATERAL MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MARGIN METHODOLOGY TRANSITION:
  - Previous: SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk) - 16 simplified scenarios
  - Current: VaR (Value at Risk) - implemented November 6, 2023
  - VaR uses 1,250+ data points for margin calculation (vs. SPAN's 16 scenarios)

VaR CALCULATION METHODS:
  1. Historical Simulation Method (HS-VaR):
     - Portfolio profit/loss obtained from scenarios based on historical market
       data
     - More market-sensitive than SPAN
  2. Alternative Simulation Method (AS-VaR):
     - Parameters (e.g., expected price fluctuations) set in advance
     - Used as fallback or supplement

MARGIN REQUIREMENTS:
  - Confidence level: At least 99% of potential future exposures
  - Calculated for each Clearing Business using appropriate methodology
  - Open positions marked-to-market using most recent price at least once daily
  - Variation margin exchanged with Clearing Participants after mark-to-market

CLEARING FUND (DEFAULT FUND):
  - Separate clearing funds for each clearing business
  - Commodity Derivatives Clearing Fund: JPY 20.4 billion (as of June 28, 2024)
  - JSCC is considering reorganization/consolidation of default fund structure
  - 2024 initial margin methodology reforms welcomed by clearing members

DEFAULT WATERFALL:
  Standard CCP waterfall structure:
  1. Defaulting member's margin
  2. Defaulting member's clearing fund contribution
  3. JSCC's own skin-in-the-game capital
  4. Non-defaulting members' clearing fund contributions
  5. Additional assessment powers

5. DVP SETTLEMENT VIA BOJ-NET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DVP (Delivery versus Payment) settlement ensures simultaneous exchange of
securities and cash, eliminating principal risk.

STRUCTURE BY PRODUCT:

A) Listed Equities, ETFs, REITs:
   - Securities leg: Book-entry transfer at JASDEC (Japan Securities
     Depository Center)
   - Cash leg: Settlement via Fund Settlement Banks
   - Method: Multilateral netting through JSCC as CCP
   - Securities transferred on net basis; cash payment netted across products

B) Listed JGBs and OTC JGBs:
   - Gross-Gross DVP settlement via BOJ RTGS (Real-Time Gross Settlement)
   - Settlement of cash and securities between Clearing Participant and JSCC
     fulfilled simultaneously on a gross basis
   - Settlement is final at the time of transfer (irrevocable)
   - BOJ acts as CSD (Central Securities Depository) for JGBs

C) OTC Derivatives (IRS, CDS):
   - All JPY settlements conducted through BOJ account
   - CDS clearing: Once per week on 3-day cycle; clearing at 16:00 on
     third business day

BOJ-NET INTEGRATION:
  - JSCC uses BOJ accounts for all clearing businesses wherever possible
  - Specifically: All JPY settlements for CDS, IRS, and OTC JGB Clearing
    Businesses are conducted through BOJ accounts
  - This provides settlement finality backed by central bank money

7. HOW JSCC WOULD NEED TO ADAPT FOR EXTENDED TRADING HOURS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If TSE were to implement extended trading hours (12-hour or 24-hour), JSCC
would face the following challenges:

A) SETTLEMENT CYCLE COMPRESSION:
   - Current T+2 batch processing assumes single daily trading window
   - Extended hours increase the volume of trades requiring settlement
   - Multiple clearing cycles per day may be needed
   - Trade date assignment becomes complex for overnight sessions

B) NETTING WINDOW EXPANSION:
   - Current netting occurs after market close (post-15:30)
   - 24-hour trading would require either:
     (a) Multiple intraday netting cycles
     (b) Continuous real-time netting
     (c) Extended batch windows with modified cutoff times

C) SETTLEMENT BANK COORDINATION:
   - Fund Settlement Banks currently process within fixed hours
   - BOJ-NET operating hours would need extension
   - JASDEC processing windows (07:00-12:30) would need expansion

D) MARGIN MANAGEMENT:
   - Intraday margin calls may be needed for overnight positions
   - VaR calculations would need to account for extended session volatility
   - Mark-to-market frequency would need to increase beyond once daily

E) FAIL MANAGEMENT:
   - Current 50% fail rate due to late instructions would worsen
   - Extended hours = more trades = more settlement complexity
   - Fail resolution processes would need acceleration

F) TECHNOLOGY INFRASTRUCTURE:
   - Batch processing windows (currently overnight) would shrink
   - System maintenance windows would be compressed
   - Real-time or near-real-time clearing capabilities may be required

  - Foreign investors have complained about this cap
  - Regulatory scrutiny of ATS caps ongoing

4. TECHNOLOGY AND MATCHING ENGINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

BUILDER: SK Inc. C&C (SK C&C)
  - Constructed entire trading system including:
    * Channel and matching engine system for transaction processing
    * Information distribution system (integrated with KRX and Korea Securities
      Depository)
    * Trading support system for statistics and management

MATCHING ENGINE:
  - High-performance engine designed for 12-hour trading sessions
  - Continuous matching with price-time priority
  - Specific latency and throughput figures not publicly disclosed

ORDER TYPES:
  - Market Orders
  - Limit Orders
  - Mid-Point Orders (NEW): Execute at average of best bid and ask prices;
    designed to reduce market impact and improve execution quality
  - Stop-Limit Orders (NEW): Triggered when stock reaches investor-specified
    price; provides conditional execution capability

CONNECTIVITY:

  - Low-latency access for international institutional investors

  - Execution fees: 20% to 40% lower than KRX
  - This competitive pricing has been a major driver of rapid adoption

6. SIGNIFICANCE FOR KRX 24-HOUR TRANSITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nextrade's rapid success has directly accelerated KRX's extended hours plans:

  - Nextrade's 30% market value share within months forced KRX to respond
  - KRX announced 12-hour trading plans (8:00-20:00) for June 2026
  - KRX announced 24-hour trading target for December 2026/2027
  - Nextrade demonstrated that Korean investors have strong demand for
    pre-market and after-market trading
  - Extended hours account for nearly 39% of Nextrade's trading value,
    validating the business case

KEY OBSERVATIONS:

1. JAPANNEXT'S NIGHT MARKET provides the most extensive extended-hours coverage
   in Asia (17:00-06:00, ~13 hours overnight), significantly exceeding both
   Nextrade and KRX's initial plans.

2. NEXTRADE'S RAPID GROWTH (0% to ~29% value share in <12 months) demonstrates
   strong Korean investor demand for extended hours, which Japan's PTS market
   took over a decade to build (Japannext: ~5% after 18+ years).

3. KRX'S AGGRESSIVE TIMELINE (Phase 2: 24-hour by December 2026) is far more
   ambitious than anything JPX has announced for cash equities. JPX appears
   content to let Japannext handle extended hours while TSE focuses on the
   core session.

4. CLEARING INFRASTRUCTURE is the binding constraint for both markets:
   - Japan: JSCC batch processing, T+2 settlement, no concrete T+1 plan
   - Korea: KSD T+2 settlement, planning T+1 alongside 24-hour transition
   - The KRX approach of combining settlement cycle compression with trading
     hour extension is more aggressive but also more risky

5. THE "DIVISION OF LABOR" MODEL (Japan) vs. "SINGLE VENUE" MODEL (KRX):
   - Japan: TSE handles core hours; Japannext handles extended hours
   - Korea: KRX plans to handle all hours itself (while Nextrade also operates)
   - Japan's model distributes risk but fragments liquidity
   - KRX's model centralizes liquidity but increases operational burden
